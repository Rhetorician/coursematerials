Project 4: Public Voice Project

Choose either the Public Discourse Project or the Future Retirement Speech. Both options ask you to communicate with an audience beyond this class.

In RWS 305W this semester, you have worked on professional documents, labor market research, rhetorical analysis, and questions about work and identity. For this final project, you will turn some of that work outward and address an audience beyond this class.

You have two options. You may complete the original Public Discourse Project, in which you advise entering SDSU students about a major and career field, or you may complete the Future Retirement Speech, in which you speak from the point of view of your future self looking back on the career you built.

Whichever option you choose, make the project for the audience you have identified and use course material where it helps you make the point.

In this option, you are asked to transmit what you have learned to an audience of your peers, that is, fellow students at SDSU who are taking their first steps toward choosing their academic majors.

Instructions

  1. Write a script for a roughly ten-minute PowerPoint presentation you would give to a group of SDSU freshmen who are interested in pursuing similar career goals and developing the skills, knowledge, and experience that will improve their future employability.
  2. Create a roughly ten-minute PowerPoint presentation consisting of eight to ten slides that presents the points listed in your script.
  3. Submit your PowerPoint along with a PDF of the integrated script to the submission link.

Your script should be single-spaced and should correspond to the text you include on the slides in your presentation.

In this option, you will write and record a retirement speech from the point of view of your future self. Imagine that you are at the end of a career or major professional chapter. Look back at the career you built and explain what mattered along the way.

Instructions

  1. Choose the occasion and audience for the speech. Your audience might include colleagues, family, younger workers, students entering your field, or people who helped shape your career.
  2. Write a speech script that looks back on the career, values, choices, setbacks, relationships, and identity you built.
  3. Use course materials to support the speech. You may draw on your Labor Market Analysis, Meg Jay's chapters, career readings, or any other relevant course materials.
  4. Record yourself delivering the speech. You may use notes, but you should not appear to be reading from a monotone script like a hostage video.
Focus: Don't make this a fantasy job speech. Show how a you moved from where you are now toward a future worth having.

Adding Speaker Notes

  1. Open your PowerPoint presentation.
  2. Go to the View tab and select Notes.
  3. In the Notes pane, type your script for each slide.

Printing Slides with Notes

  1. Go to the File menu and select Print.
  2. In the Settings section, select Notes Pages to print each slide with its speaker notes.
  3. Select Print to PDF. For Mac users, this appears at the bottom of the print dialog screen. For PC users, select PDF as your printer.

You may use any recording tool, but the finished speech must be posted to YouTube.
