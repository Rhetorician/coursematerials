Project 4: Public Voice Project

In RWS 305W this semester, you have worked on professional documents, labor market research, rhetorical analysis, and questions about work, identity, and career preparation. For this final project, you will turn that work outward and address an audience beyond this class.

You have two options. You may complete the original Public Discourse Project, in which you advise entering SDSU students about a major and career field, or you may complete the Future Retirement Speech, in which you speak from the point of view of your future self looking back on the career, values, choices, and identity you built.

Both options require an audience, a purpose, and course materials.

Public Discourse Project

In this option, you are asked to transmit what you have learned to an audience of your peers, that is, fellow students at SDSU who are taking their first steps toward choosing their academic majors.

Instructions

  1. Write a script for a roughly ten-minute PowerPoint presentation you would give to a group of SDSU freshmen who are interested in choosing the major you have selected and developing their skills, knowledge, and experience in this field to improve their future employability.
  2. Create a roughly ten-minute PowerPoint presentation consisting of eight to ten slides that presents the points listed in your script.
  3. Submit your PowerPoint along with a PDF of the integrated script to the submission link.

Your script should be single-spaced and should correspond to the text you include on the slides in your presentation.

Future Retirement Speech

In this option, you will write and record a retirement speech from the point of view of your future self. Imagine that you are at the end of a career or major professional chapter. Your speech should explain what you built, what guided you, what tested you, and what you hope others understand from your example.

Instructions

  1. Choose the occasion and audience for the speech. Your audience might include colleagues, family, younger workers, students entering your field, or people who helped shape your career.
  2. Write a speech script that looks back on the career, values, choices, setbacks, relationships, and identity you built.
  3. Use course materials to support the speech. You may draw on your Labor Market Analysis, Meg Jay's chapters, career readings, or any other relevant course materials.
  4. Record yourself delivering the speech. You may use notes, but you should not appear to be reading from a monotone script like a hostage video.
Focus: This is not a fantasy job speech. It should show how a person moves from where they are now toward a future worth having.

Guidelines

Whichever option you choose, your project should address a specific audience and use course materials for a specific purpose. You should discuss work, identity, career preparation, labor market conditions, skills, knowledge, experience, and identity capital where they are relevant to your project.

In addition to the materials of this module, you may draw from any other resources from Modules 1-3. You do not need to use every resource, but you should demonstrate that you have consulted a variety of them.

Slides, visuals, examples, and stories should support the message.

Adding Speaker Notes

  1. Open your PowerPoint presentation.
  2. Go to the View tab and select Notes.
  3. In the Notes pane, type your script for each slide.

Printing Slides with Notes

  1. Go to the File menu and select Print.
  2. In the Settings section, select Notes Pages to print each slide with its speaker notes.
  3. Select Print to PDF. For Mac users, this appears at the bottom of the print dialog screen. For PC users, select PDF as your printer.
