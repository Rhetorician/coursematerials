Choose the kind of article that can answer the question

“Article” is not one source type. The useful distinction is what the source can actually tell you.

Scholarly does not automatically mean “better.”

Choose the type that can answer the factor you are investigating. A current trade or business article may be more useful than an older scholarly article when the question is about what is happening now.

Business news and reporting

Best for what is happening now: a company decision, controversy, regulation, market change, competitor move, labor problem, lawsuit, customer reaction, or other current development.

  • Current events and recent changes
  • Independent reporting
  • Quotes from companies, workers, regulators, analysts, or customers

Trade and industry articles

Best for what people inside an industry are seeing, doing, debating, or preparing for.

  • Industry practices and operating problems
  • Technology, labor, regulation, and market trends
  • Examples from comparable organizations

Scholarly articles

Best when the factor needs research evidence rather than only current reporting.

  • Studies, data, methods, and findings
  • Customer or employee behavior
  • Longer-term evidence about effects, attitudes, access, risk, or outcomes

Read the database language

The Navigator's searches use ordinary Boolean search tools. You do not need to memorize a secret librarian dialect. You do need to know what the pieces are doing so you can change them when the results suck.

AND

Requires both ideas. Use it to connect different concepts: Starbucks AND homelessness.

OR

Connects alternatives or synonyms: (homelessness OR "unhoused people").

" "

Searches a phrase as a phrase: "customer service".

( )

Keeps alternatives together: (regulation OR policy OR compliance).

*

Searches a word stem. consumer* can retrieve consumer and consumers. Databases vary slightly in how truncation works.

Use NOT carefully. It removes a term, but it can eliminate useful results along with the junk you were trying to remove. Treat it as a cleanup tool, not a default search operator.

Example from the Navigator

("McDonald's" OR "fast food restaurant" OR "quick service restaurant")
AND ("plant-based meat" OR "meat alternatives" OR "vegetarian menu options")
AND (consumer* OR customer* OR demand)

The first group identifies the organization or industry. The second identifies the change or issue. The third tells the database what kind of evidence the student wants for the selected factor.

Repair a search that is not working

Database searching is iterative. A bad first result is information about the search, not evidence that your topic is impossible.

Search Repair Lab

Paste the search you are using, then choose the problem you are having.

Operating rule: Change one thing at a time. If you rewrite the whole search after every weak result, you will not know which change helped.

    Advanced article searching
    
      
    Once the basic search is working, database filters and careful keyword changes can make the results much more useful.
    

      
    
        
    
          
    
            
    Use the database filters after you have useful keywords.
    Publication date, subject, publication type, peer review, and other filters can narrow a strong search. They cannot rescue meaningless search terms.
    
            
    Read useful result records for better vocabulary.
    Subject terms, keywords, headlines, abstracts, and industry language can give you better search terms than the words you started with.
    
            
    Run more than one search.
    Your Recommendation Report has multiple factors. One giant search is usually worse than several focused searches aimed at different evidence.

    Before you keep the article
    
      
    An article is useful when it gives you evidence for the factor you are investigating. Mentioning your company, industry, or topic is not enough.
    

      
    
        
    Which factor does it help investigate?
    You should be able to connect the article to one of the factors your group is actually researching.
    
        
    What evidence does it give you?
    Name the fact, finding, example, comparison, data point, or expert information you could actually use.
    
        
    Is it current enough for the claim?
    Recent developments usually need recent evidence. Older research can still be useful when it explains a pattern that remains relevant.
    
        
    Does it answer the question?
    An article that merely mentions the topic is not automatically evidence for the factor you are investigating.
    
      
    

      
    
        
    Finish this sentence: “This article helps us investigate ______ because it shows ______.” If you cannot fill in both blanks specifically, keep searching.
