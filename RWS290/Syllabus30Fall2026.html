Course Description

RWS 290 is a business communication course that builds on the rhetorical principles students discover and practice in earlier 100 and 200-level courses, such as attention to audience, purpose, presentation, evidence, research, and language. As an intermediate writing course dedicated to business pre-majors, RWS 290 students develop, compose, and evaluate the forms of communication frequently encountered in professional settings (e.g., letters, memos, presentations, reports). In other words, students will respond to academic tasks and practice communication in a wide variety of genres that require research methods and communication styles specific to business.

STUDENT LEARNING OUTCOMES

At the end of this course, students should be able to:

  • Produce letters, reports, proposals and other business documents that are organized, communicate clearly, use appropriate technology and address the intended audiences, purposes and contexts;
  • Craft and deliver professional presentations;
  • Collaborate in a dedicated team using effective interpersonal communication skills;
  • Use a clear, concise and appropriate writing style to summarize issues and support decision making by others;
  • Collect, evaluate, analyze, organize, interpret and present data in written and oral messages;
  • Edit to eliminate errors in punctuation, spelling and grammar in order to avoid misunderstanding and create professional communication.

course Assumptions

Rhetoric and Business

Because business professionals convey information to non-business audiences and others in their fields, they must understand the communication requirements for different groups. For example:

  • What does an accountant understand in an audit that an executive might not?
  • Why do managers and marketing professionals look at proposals differently?
  • Why is it important to understand the role of technical content in formal and informal business presentations?

These questions suggest that your audience for business communication will likely have its own motivation, goals, and level of expertise. The better you understand this, the better your chances of successfully communicating important ideas.

Throughout the semester, we will address the “rhetorical triad”—the concepts of audience, purpose, and exigency—as a way to improve all the business documents and presentations you create and evaluate.

Verbal Communication

We will also emphasize verbal communication. In job postings and performance reviews, managers always stress the importance of a candidate’s ability to speak well in various situations. Your “verbal confidence” can be your career’s catalyst—or its hindrance. Throughout this course we will incorporate verbal communication strategies into exercises, short presentations, and group work. By the end of the course, you should be more comfortable speaking to individuals and groups.

Collaboration

This course assumes that professionals never work alone. They refer to precedents; they connect with other business professionals; they lead design teams. They explain complicated information to clients, customers, executives, subject matter experts and interested members of the public, who may have no background in their fields of expertise. You will discover very quickly that the most highly valued managers, marketing professionals, financial analysts, real estate brokers, hotel HR representatives and information systems administrators are those who work effectively in varied teams. The course will provide a theoretical framework for writing-as-collaboration, and specific course projects and assignments will support that framework.

ASSIGNMENT DESCRIPTIONS

Project 1: Resume

This module contains one major deliverable, a resume, which you will write in response to a job announcement for a position you are interested in applying for this year.

Project 2: Bad News Message

This module contains one major project, in which you will learn how to deliver negative news in a way that promotes your business interests.

Project 3: Persuasive Message

This module contains one major project, in which you practice prompting action from a targeted audience in a business setting.

Project 4: Design Charrette

This module contains one major assignment, in which you will learn the basic principles of document design including visual integration and proper citation formatting practices.

Culminating Project: Recommendation Report

The Recommendation Report requires you to draw from the skills practiced throughout the semester and recommend a course of action to address a current business problem. The project includes several smaller assignments that help your group manage the research, writing, presentation, and revision process.

Course Policies

Course Culture

This is a course in collaborative written and verbal communication, and you must endeavor to participate in our sessions as much as you can. I assume that you are strong students and emerging business professionals, and I run this course like a series of professional business meetings. As such, I expect your attention and engagement while I lead discussions. Typically, I present information for a portion of our meeting, then break everyone out to work on assignments together or individually. These supplemental assignments relate directly to material we discuss each time we meet and will contribute tangibly to your final projects. In our meetings, you should share appropriate ideas and observations from your academic and professional lives as appropriate.

Course Readings

Complete all assigned Zinsser chapters and module materials before the class meeting listed in the schedule. You are responsible for the assigned material even when we do not discuss every part in class.

Quizzes

Four Guidance Quizzes are assigned during the semester. Each quiz covers the module guidance and the Zinsser chapters listed in the schedule. Each quiz occurs during the same week as the related final submission.

Writing Assignment/Project Requirements

We will review specific requirements for assignments and projects in class, and you will find project descriptions, handouts, samples, and other supplemental material for each deliverable on Canvas. Submit written work on the date indicated on the calendar. All documents (resumes, letters, memos, visuals, reports) must be prepared with appropriate software and must conform to the conventions of the genre. In other words, follow the directions I provide exactly, to the letter. If you submit a document that varies significantly from the specified format, I may ask you to resubmit it.

Evaluating Your Work

Each document or presentation you prepare reflects your knowledge and establishes your professional image. I make every effort to evaluate assignments and projects quickly, but I may take extra time assessing your resumes for reasons I will discuss during an early meeting. I use comprehensive rubrics when I grade your work and will go over each one when I introduce an assignment or project.

Assignment grades are determined by the published criteria and my judgment. Final course grades are based on the total points earned. There is no course curve. I strongly encourage you to meet me during office hours if you have questions about a grade. (Note: If you are taking RWS 290 for C/NC, you must complete all projects for credit.)

Late Work

The pace of our course demands that you submit all assignments by their due dates so that you can move on to the next assignment, and so I can comment on and evaluate your work in a reasonable timeframe. As a result, I cannot accept late work unless you can present a dire, documented reason why you need an extension.

If serious illness or personal catastrophe threatens to compromise your ability to meet course criteria, however, please let me know in a timely manner. Usually, adjustments can be made and solutions can be reached, unless you wait too long to deal with your situation.

Once a submission due date has passed, there are no rewrites, but you may revise and rewrite as much as you wish up until that date. In such intensive courses as this one, time for revisions cannot be generous. We will adhere to revision timeframes and focused revision strategies throughout the course. See the Course Calendar for more information about the revision timelines for our projects.

GOOGLE DOC POLICY

All work for every assignment in this course must be completed entirely within a single Google Document created specifically for that assignment. You are required to give mfeigner@sdsu.edu full Editor access to this document from the moment it is created.

  • Each individual assignment, including the resume, bad news message, persuasive message, and design charrette, requires its own separate Google Doc. For the Recommendation Report, each group must use one shared Google Doc for each written deliverable unless I direct otherwise.
  • All research, drafting, revising, editing, thinking, and development of the assignment must take place exclusively inside this one document.
  • Using multiple Google Docs for the same assignment is prohibited.
  • All submissions must be made by sharing the single, complete Google Doc with Editor access to the submission link on Canvas.

Please note that failure to follow any part of this policy will result in an automatic zero (0) on the assignment. This grade is final and non-negotiable. No revisions or make-up opportunities will be allowed for violations.

By submitting work in this course, you acknowledge and agree to comply with this policy.

Academic Honesty

Academic Honesty: The University adheres to a strict policy prohibiting cheating and plagiarism. Examples of academic dishonesty include but are not limited to:

  • copying, in part or in whole, from another's test or other examination;
  • obtaining copies of a test, an examination, or other course material
    without the permission of the instructor;
  • collaborating with another or others in work to be presented without the permission of the instructor;
  • falsifying records, laboratory work, or other course data;
  • submitting work previously presented in another course, if contrary to the rules of the course;
  • altering or interfering with grading procedures;
  • assisting another student in any of the above;
  • using sources verbatim or paraphrasing without giving proper attribution (this can include phrases, sentences, paragraphs and/or pages of work);
  • copying and pasting work from an online or offline source directly and calling it your own;
  • using information you find from an online or offline source without giving the author credit;
  • replacing words or phrases from another source and inserting your own words or phrases.

The California State University system requires instructors to report all instances of academic misconduct to the Center for Student Rights and Responsibilities. Academic dishonesty will result in disciplinary review by the University and may lead to probation, suspension, or expulsion. Instructors may also, at their discretion, penalize student grades on any assignment or assessment discovered to have been produced in an academically dishonest manner.

FAQs

QUESTIONS ABOUT GRADING

QUESTION

Why did I get a 0 on my work?

ANSWER

If you were assigned a 0 without an explanation in the comment box it’s almost certainly because you did not follow the Google Doc policy outlined in this syllabus.

QUESTIONS ABOUT CANVAS

QUESTION

How do I see feedback on my work?

ANSWER

First, written feedback on Canvas comes in two forms: comments and annotations. Comments are located under the “comments” section, and they are external to the document. If I commented on your work, it will usually be a summative remark(s) connected to my annotations, which are on the document itself. To view that, you must click on the document itself and hover your mouse over the bubbles that are present (see below). In the case of Google Docs, they will appear as comments that you can review at will. Note: Do not delete my comments until after the semester concludes.

QUESTIONS ABOUT DUE DATES

QUESTION

My internet went out right when I was about to submit my homework assignment. By the time I got to the library to use their computer, I had missed the deadline for submission. Will this be a problem?

ANSWER

Sticking to due dates and times is important in this course. For some assignments, however, if you are a day or two late in submitting, you should be able to proceed with the course easily enough and you will not receive a grade reduction. You do not need to email me if you’re within this grace period. (Note: Canvas will label your submission late; but again, if you submit within the above-mentioned timeframe, you may simply ignore the alert.)
