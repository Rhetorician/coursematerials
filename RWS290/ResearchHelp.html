When to get help

A research consultation is useful when you cannot tell what kind of source would answer the question.

Your searches keep missing

You know what you are trying to investigate, but the databases keep returning irrelevant results or you are unsure which database to use.

You need a source that is hard to find

The source you need may be in a different source family than the ones you have already searched.

Your evidence does not answer the factor

You have sources, but they are not helping you answer the question your report needs to investigate. A librarian can help you rethink the search.

What to bring to a consultation

A librarian can help much faster if the group arrives with the research problem in front of them.

Bring the report setup

  • Your current problem and purpose statements.
  • The factor you are trying to investigate.
  • The organization, industry, population, or location involved.
  • Any limits that matter, such as geography, time period, or organization type.

Bring what you already tried

  • A few searches that failed or returned junk.
  • The databases or websites you already used.
  • One or two sources that are close to what you need.
  • The Research Navigator search terms if you used them.

You do not need to have the research figured out before asking for help. The useful thing to bring is a specific research problem and enough context for someone else to see where the search is breaking down.
